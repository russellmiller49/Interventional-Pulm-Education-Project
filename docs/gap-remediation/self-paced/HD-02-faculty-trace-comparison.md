## B. New learner copy on these tracings (pending review)

**Hints** — shown on request, before the labels; they must not name the place.

- RV and PA: “Similar systolic peaks do not name the place. Look at diastole: where the pressure sits between beats, which way it slopes, and whether a notch interrupts the downstroke.”
- RA, wedge and the variant: “Time each small peak against the ECG. Is there a peak just after the P wave, before the QRS, and another just after the QRS? Or do the peaks arrive later, the first one only after the QRS?”
- Overdamped display: “Before naming any place, ask whether this display can be read: are the upstroke, the peak and any notch sharp enough to use?”

**Contrasts** — shown with the comparison, and in the feedback when a learner chooses the paired reading.

- RV ↔ PA: “The systolic peaks are alike, so the peak cannot separate these two. Diastole does: the right ventricle falls to a low pressure that may rise gradually through filling, with no notch; the pulmonary artery holds a higher diastolic pressure, shows a valve-closure notch on the downstroke, and runs off downward through diastole.”
- RA ↔ wedge: “Both are low-amplitude atrial shapes. Timing against the ECG separates them: the right-atrial a wave follows the P wave and a c wave follows the QRS, while the wedge waves arrive later — its a wave after the QRS, with no c wave, and typically a v wave larger than the a wave.”
- Level-matched RA ↔ wedge: the same, opening “Both are low-amplitude atrial shapes at the same mean pressure, so the number cannot separate them.”
- Overdamped display ↔ PA: the challenge's authored “What you see” and “Why no chamber can be named” text, joined.

**Feedback after a checked reading**

- Headline: “That matches: this is …” / “Not this one: this is …” / “Shown without an answer: this is …”, with the reading as “the right atrium (CVP)”, “the right ventricle”, “the pulmonary artery”, “the pulmonary capillary wedge” or “a display that cannot name a place”.
- A place chosen on the overdamped display: “The shape still looks like a place, but on this display it cannot be trusted to name one.”
- “Cannot be named” chosen on a clean tracing: “No display fault is drawn on this model tracing, so its shape can name the place.”
- The paired reading chosen: “The … is the easy confusion here.” followed by the contrast.
- Any other reading: “Read the shape against the labels now drawn on it, then compare it with the tracing it is most easily confused with.”

## C. Automated agreement checks (evidence, not approval)

`src/features/icu-hemodynamics/__tests__/recognition-practice.test.tsx` reads the SVG path the practice actually renders with its labels shown, and the positions of the drawn landmark markers and ECG markers:

- **A1:** exactly one local maximum per beat, at the RVSP marker; falling pressure at “rapid fall”; rising pressure at “up-sloping diastole”.
- **A2:** two maxima per beat (systolic peak and the wave after the notch); a local minimum at the dicrotic-notch marker followed by the dicrotic wave; falling pressure at “down-sloping diastole”; lowest diastolic pressure above A1's on the shared axis.
- **A4, A5:** maxima at the a, c and v markers and minima at x and y, each within a twentieth of a beat; the a peak after the P marker; the c peak after QRS and before T; a taller than v.
- **A3:** only two maxima per beat, at a and v; minima at x and y; no c marker; a after QRS and before T; v after T; v taller than a.
- **A5 vs A4:** every drawn point moved by one constant, at identical x; drawn mean within 2 px (about 0.3 mmHg on the 0–20 mmHg axis) of A3's.
- **A6 vs A2:** no landmark markers; narrower pulse; shallower notch; mean within a fifth of A2's pulse.

These confirm that the labels sit on the features the model draws. They do not establish that the model's shapes or numbers are clinically realistic.

## D. Existing observations for review, unchanged by HD-02

- **Timing magnitude — faculty-review hold.** The figure draws one beat per second. With the P wave at 0.84 of the cycle, the drawn P-to-a interval is about 100 ms on the right atrium (a at 0.94) and about 300 ms on the wedge (a at 0.14 of the next cycle), where the atlas annotations say about 80 ms and about 240 ms. The order and the delay the contrast relies on hold; the magnitudes differ. - **Rhythm.** The ECG strip is one idealized sinus rhythm for every tracing; atrial-fibrillation and other rhythm-specific ECGs are not modelled (already stated on the abnormal-patterns card).
- **Axis per pair.** RA, wedge and the variant use the atlas's 0–20 mmHg axis; RV, PA and the damped display use 0–40 mmHg. Each comparison shares one axis, but the axis itself distinguishes atrial from ventricular/arterial shapes at a glance.
