## RB-CORE-INT-001 — Synthetic internal mechanics (verbatim prompt)

Use this prompt **verbatim**, appended to the global base. Drive from a
validated synthetic airway-training visualization; do not present as a real
patient.

```
Synthetic medically reviewed 3D airway-training visualization, not a real patient.
A simplified polypoid intraluminal lesion partially obstructs a central airway.
The normal airway wall and the intended lumen are clearly distinguishable. A
straight rigid bronchoscope with a defined beveled distal edge is centered at the
intended tumor-lumen interface. The operator uses a short controlled axial advance
combined with slow deliberate rotation. The beveled edge separates a central
portion of the synthetic lesion while the normal airway wall remains stationary.
The operator pauses, withdraws, removes the mobile fragment, suctions debris,
recenters, and reinspects. No gore, no uncontrolled bleeding, no blind
advancement, and no bending of the rigid barrel.
```

- View: synthetic internal. Source: validated 3D visualization.
- Overlay: **"Synthetic procedural visualization"** (persistent, full duration).
- Emphasis to preserve: lesion predominantly **intraluminal**; **normal wall
  stationary**; short controlled advance; controlled rotation; pause →
  withdraw → remove fragment → suction → reinspect.

## RB-CORE-EXT-001 — External mechanics

External framing of the operator's hands and the rigid barrel. Shows the body
mechanics of a short controlled coring motion **without** claiming to show the
internal cutting result (that is the synthetic internal view's job).

```
External documentary framing of the operator's gloved hands and the straight
rigid bronchoscope at the mouth of the adult airway manikin. The operator
demonstrates the external body mechanics of coring: a short, controlled axial
advance combined with slow, deliberate rotation of the rigid barrel, hands stable
and in continuous contact with the instrument. The barrel remains perfectly
straight and constant in diameter. Dental protection is present over the upper
incisors and is not used as a fulcrum. Movement is restrained and deliberate,
with no forceful thrust.
```

- View: external. Camera: fixed documentary; hands, barrel, and mouth in frame.
- No internal anatomy claimed; pairs with RB-CORE-INT-001 in postproduction.

## RB-CORE-EXT-002 — Recover, remove, reinspect (external)

```
External framing. After the short controlled coring motion, the operator pauses,
withdraws the rigid barrel slightly under continued hand control, and a mobile
fragment is removed with a grasping instrument handed off from the assistant. The
barrel stays straight and constant in diameter throughout. Deliberate, unhurried
movement; dental protection present; no fulcrum on the teeth.
```

- View: external. Reinforces fragment removal and reinspection discipline.

## Safety framing for narration (authored separately)

- "Coring uses a short, controlled advance — never a forceful thrust."
- "The beveled edge engages the lesion; the normal airway wall should remain
  stationary. If the wall moves with the lesion, stop."
- "Pause, remove the mobile fragment, suction, recenter, and reinspect before any
  further advance."
- "This representation simplifies the anatomy to demonstrate the movement
  relationship."

## Hard rejections specific to coring

- Airway wall moving together with the lesion.
- Any bending or diameter change of the rigid barrel during the coring motion.
- Blind advancement, or advancement continuing after loss of the distal lumen.
- Uncontrolled or excessive rotation.
- Gore, or bleeding that obscures the tissue plane (unless a separate,
  clinically reviewed synthetic complication illustration is explicitly
  commissioned).
