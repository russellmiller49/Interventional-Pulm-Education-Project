## Statements for the owner to confirm

1. **The orientation rule.** The new screen teaches: held square in front of the operator, the
   scope puts the side of the airway facing away from the operator at 12 o'clock. From that one
   rule: at the head of the bed anterior is at 12 and the patient's right is on the right; facing
   the patient the picture is half a turn round; at the patient's right bedside, looking across the
   bed, the patient's left is at 12 and anterior at 3; at the patient's left bedside the patient's
   right is at 12 and anterior at 9. The head-of-bed and facing views are the standard teaching.
   The two bedside views follow from the rule and assume the operator faces across the bed.
2. **The landmark table** on that screen, at the head of the bed: right upper lobe at 3 o'clock
   from the right main bronchus, middle lobe at 12 from the bronchus intermedius, RB6 at 6, left
   upper lobe at 9 from the left main bronchus, LB6 at 6. Every other row is this one turned.
3. **The survey no longer has a narrowed segment**, so the simulator no longer exercises "not
   reachable". The three statuses are still taught on the screen before the task and asked for in
   the section's questions.
4. **The left-lung start view** was left as it is (left main bronchus, mid-way, upright): both lobar
   openings are in view there. Say if a set angulation is wanted, as for the middle lobe.
5. **The left main bronchus still** is already a view from above the carina and was not changed.
6. **The plain goal wording** goes beyond what the slides named. "All the goals for this task are
   met." is close to the "Every goal on this card is met" line an earlier review (BF-PRE-REVIEW-01)
   had replaced with "Recorded: every step this card asks for…". The older wording is still in the
   code for a first-contract section and can be restored for all sections by one flag
   (`plainGoals` in `BronchStageHost.tsx`, `plain` in the pane).

## Top 3 teaching improvements

1. The survey asks the question that matters, "which segment is this?", at the moment the fellow is
   inside it, instead of asking for a status in a table out of sight.
2. Driving now tests where the fellow chose to go, not how exactly the tip was aimed: a deliberate
   bend toward an opening is enough, and a wrong turn is retraced rather than penalised.
3. Orientation is taught as one rule with a picture that turns, so a fellow who moves from the head
   of the bed to the bedside knows why the right main bronchus is no longer on the right.
