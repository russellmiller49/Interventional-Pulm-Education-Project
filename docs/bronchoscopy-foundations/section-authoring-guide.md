# Bronchoscopy Foundations — section authoring guide

This guide is for whoever writes one Learn section of `/bronchoscopy-foundations` (a person or an
agent). Read all of it before writing. The course is a rebuild of the introduction to flexible
bronchoscopy on the shared lesson stage used by the ECMO, ventilation, circulation, hemodynamics and
peripheral-imaging modules. Every section is **data**: one file validated at import and turned into
stage steps by an adapter. You do not write React.

## 1. What you write, and how you know it holds

- **One file:** `src/features/bronchoscopy-foundations/content/sections/<section-id>.ts`, exporting
  `export const section: BronchSectionDefinition`. Touch no other file. If a shared registry
  (spine, control panel, grammar, tree, media, landmarks, sources, register) looks wrong, say so in
  your final report instead of editing it.
- **The contract:** `src/features/bronchoscopy-foundations/content/types.ts`. Read every comment.
- **The exemplar:** `content/sections/shared-airway.ts`. Match its shape, voice, density and
  source discipline. It passes every check.
- **Validation, until it prints ✓:**
  ```bash
  npx tsx scripts/bronchoscopy-foundations/check-section.ts <section-id>
  ```
- **Types, until your file has no errors** (other in-progress sections may show errors; look only
  at yours):
  ```bash
  npx tsc -p scripts/bronchoscopy-foundations/tsconfig.sections.json 2>&1 | grep "sections/<section-id>.ts"
  ```

The validator enforces the mechanical rules (§6). It cannot judge clinical accuracy, leakage in
paraphrase, or whether a transfer is a real transfer. Those are yours, and an independent reviewer
will check them against the rubric in §7.

## 4. Writing rules

**Voice.** Plain clinical prose for physicians beginning bronchoscopy training. Clinical term first,
then the precise statement, then (only as an aid) the analogy. Short sentences. No motivational
filler, no "Great job", no second person imperatives stacked in lists. British or American spelling
consistently American except where the exemplar already uses a term.

**Vocabulary the build refuses** (the shared learner-copy gate): score, points, grade, percent, `%`,
pass/passed, fail/failed, mastery, exam, test, quiz, assessment, certification, certified, competent,
competency, correct, incorrect, wrong, route, seed, engine, query, reducer. Say instead: "decision
held" / "did not hold", "check", "study" or "assay" (a laboratory test), "evaluation", "approach"
or "path" (not route), "advance beyond" or "distal to" (not pass), "competence" (allowed),
concentrations in **mg/mL** (never a percentage), fractions in words ("about half", "a quarter").
If a device term genuinely requires a refused word (for example the scope's leak check is widely
called a "leak test"), prefer a paraphrase ("leak check"); if you cannot, add a `copyExemptions`
entry with the reason on that block or item only.

**Digits.** Titles, short titles, headings, recognize titles, act/observe/explain step titles and
micro-case presentation titles carry **no digits** (no "RB6" in a title: say "the superior segment
of the right lower lobe"). Body text, rationales and stems may use airway codes (RB6, LB4+5) and
authored teaching numbers.

**Numbers.** Never invent a threshold, dose, volume, aliquot, return fraction, time, device
dimension, pressure or setting. Where the spec leaves a value to local policy or the device's
instructions, say so and list the `localPolicyIds`. Teach direction and change from this patient's
own baseline. Educational arithmetic (the D19 ledger, the ideal-circle area relation) is labelled
as arithmetic, never as a dose or sizing rule.

**The review register wins.** Examples the build refuses outright: "follow the dark", a 90-second
survey as a standard, "no suction before BAL" as an absolute, a universal lavage volume or return
threshold, relabelling a sample to change a culture cutoff, a colony count or susceptibility value,
pressure-control breaths that "end when the set pressure is reached", "always suction" / "always
withdraw" / "the opposite side first" as bleeding rules, blind blocker placement, a tube-to-stenosis
multiplier, drug doses or rescue doses, dexmedetomidine "without respiratory depression", ketamine
"contraindicated", billing or procedure codes, PEG instructions, "as shown in the video". A
distractor may state a refused instruction-level phrase only if its rationale refutes it and the
item lists a `registerExemptions` entry naming the R-item.

**Sources.** Every block, item, sort, identify set, sequence, ledger, report and scenario carries
`sourceRefs` and a `claimClass`:

- `source` — the three uploaded works (S1 textbook, S2 training manual, S3 faculty manual), cited by
  PDF pages from the spec's brackets;
- `transcript-source` — a lecture transcript T01–T16, cited by the spec's time span (the host prints
  the fixed transcript sentence with it);
- `update` — U1–U13, cited by the section label the spec gives;
- `synthesis` — the spec's SYNTHESIS paragraphs, cited to the sources they integrate;
- `design` — course design (may stand without a source);
- `local-policy` — depends on the institution or the device's instructions (name the policies);
- `review-flag` — a statement qualified by a register item (name it in `reviewItemIds`).
  Use the class the spec gives the paragraph you draw from.

**Items.** Three or four choices, ids `a`–`d`, exactly one `best`. At least two distractors are
documented wrong mental models (`incorrect-mechanism`) or the harmful reflex (`unsafe`); a
defensible half-answer is `reasonable-but-incomplete`. The keyed label must be no more than a
quarter longer than the longest distractor — and across the course the key should usually **not**
be the longest option (write a long, specific distractor). No absolutes in option labels ("always",
"never", "necessarily", "guarantees", "every time"). Each rationale explains the choice in terms of
the observation (≥ 40 characters), never "this is wrong". The explanation (2–4 sentences) states
the mechanism. A patient item has a `situation`; a stem is one short question.

**Adapting a manifest seed.** Keep its id (`Q05`) and `seedId`, keep its objective, and reword as
the gate and the rules require: concentrations to mg/mL, absolutes replaced by the concrete wrong
move, the key no longer the longest, "what should be avoided" reframed as "what is the next move".
The spec prints every seed with option-level feedback (lines 1296–1748); use that feedback in the
rationales. Q12 (completion) and Q32 (authoring) are not learner items. Q28–Q31 belong to the
optional extensions and are not used this round.

**New items.** Use the N-id your section is assigned in §5 (`N02`…`N12`), or
`<section-id>-predict` / `<section-id>-transfer` when none is assigned. Item ids are unique across
the whole course.

**One new concept.** `newConcept` is one idea. Other objectives the section homes are carried by
blocks, the Act, the transfer and practice, but the section is built around the one idea.

**Transfer.** A different situation testing the same principle (another airway, a rotated image, a
different phase of the procedure, a different team member, a ventilated patient) — never the same
stem re-nouned. `transferVariant` says what changed. When the transfer deliberately retrieves an
earlier section's idea, set `retrievesFrom`.

**Harmful reflex.** Name it in `harmfulReflex`, make it the `unsafe` choice where it fits, and make
sure the Act cannot be completed by it (a scope-lab goal with a `without` test, a scenario frame
whose unsafe choice is refused).

**Practice micro-cases.** One decision, a static situation ("the signals in the room"), a title that
names the situation and never the mechanism. Adapt a manifest case when §5 assigns one (use its
facts, keep `manifestCaseId`), otherwise author an `mc-<slug>`. **Never** use a capstone case
(C01, C04, C05, C06, C07, C09, C10, C16) or an extension case (C13, C18–C20, C22–C24) as practice or
in any section item; C21 may be used only where §5 says so.

**Objectives.** List every objective your section is the home of (§5), each with the observable
subtask in this section that addresses it and its evidence method. Use
`observed-physical-skill-required` for hand skills (the app can teach the idea but cannot see the
hands) and add `physicalSkillNote`; use `not-app-assessable` only for genuinely interpersonal or
institutional objectives, and still name the subtask that teaches toward it. Prefer a committed
item, an Act task or a practice case over "not app assessable".

**Say where.** Every instruction names the surface it means, using the names in
`content/landmarks.ts` (Steps panel: "the statements to place on this card", "the answer choices on
this card", …; Simulator panel: "the bronchoscope view", "the airway map", "the scope controls under
the view", "the inspection record", "the monitor", …; Teaching panel: "What this section is for",
"What this section adds", "The idea in one picture", "The five controls", "Reading the view", "What
this model leaves out", or one of your own block headings).

## 6. What the validator enforces

Titles without digits; the copy gate and the register on every string (rationales may carry an
exempted, refuted instruction phrase); valid module, objective, drill, seed and case ids;
prerequisites earlier in the order; three or more blocks opening with a `question` block, at least
one `normal-reference` or `worked-example`, one `common-errors`, one `after-commitment`; unique
block ids and headings; valid sources for every claim class; valid media ids; three or four choices
with one `best`, two wrong mental models, the key no more than a quarter longer than the longest
distractor, no absolutes in labels, rationales of forty characters or more; deny patterns that match
the key or its explanation and never match a pre-commit surface; the control strip's verdict agreeing
with its states; landmarks that exist on the named pane; act-specific shapes (every sort origin used,
identify answers among its choices, ledger totals agreeing with unknown rows, report fields with
supported and unsupported options, scenario frames with one best choice, scope-lab goals that are
not met by doing nothing); practice for every mechanism and application section.

## 7. The review rubric your section will be read against

1. **Clinical accuracy and source fidelity** — every statement traceable to the cited spec paragraph
   and its bracketed citation; register adopted treatments used; no invented numbers; local-policy
   dependencies named.
2. **Leakage** — nothing on a pre-commit surface gives the key, the mechanism or the best action,
   including by paraphrase or by elimination (a title that names the answer's category, a signal
   list that only fits one choice).
3. **Cueing** — the key is not identifiable by length, specificity, hedging, grammar or position;
   distractors are real misconceptions with honest rationales.
4. **Safety-scoring consistency** — the harmful reflex is `unsafe` and cannot complete the Act; no
   simulated improvement from an action the copy calls unsafe.
5. **Sequencing** — nothing depends on a later section; normal before broken; one new concept.
6. **Terminology** — one term per concept, the tree's names (`airwayTree.ts`), the five controls'
   names (`controlPanel.ts`), grammar rows referenced by id rather than restated.
7. **Transfer** — a changed situation, not a re-nouned stem.
8. **Say where** — every instruction names a surface that exists.
9. **Copy density** — plain, short, physician-level; no filler.
