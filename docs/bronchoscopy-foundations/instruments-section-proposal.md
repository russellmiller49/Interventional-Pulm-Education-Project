# Proposal: a section on the scope and its tools

Status: for the owner to decide (2026-11-03). Nothing here is built. The instrument lab now sits on
one screen of `biopsy-and-specimens` ("Work each tool through the channel"), which puts that section
at its twelve-minute cap. This proposal gives the scope and its tools a section of their own, taught
before the fellow first drives the scope.

## Placement

First in the Handle phase, before `five-controls`: Prepare → **The scope and its tools** → Five
controls → Larynx and entry. Proposed id `scope-and-tools`.

## Objectives that would move

| Objective | From                   | Asks                                                         |
| --------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| M03-O1    | `pre-use-check`        | Name the control section, bending section, channel, valves   |
| M03-O2    | `pre-use-check`        | Choose a scope and accessory pair and say its limits         |
| M03-O5    | `pre-use-check`        | Tell the working channel from the outer diameter             |
| M13-O1    | `biopsy-and-specimens` | Check fit and the protected state before channel passage     |
| M13-O4    | `biopsy-and-specimens` | Stop unsafe withdrawal; handling is not procedure competence |
| E01-O2    | extension (optional)   | Graspers, baskets, balloons, cryoadhesion                    |

`pre-use-check` keeps M03-O3 and M03-O4; `biopsy-and-specimens` keeps M13-O2, M13-O3 and M14.

## Screens

1. **Hook.** "Will this tool reach the target, fit the channel and come back safely?"
2. **Prediction.** A tool chosen for a channel it does not fit.
3. **The parts; fiberscope and videoscope.** The parts photograph and its naming task move here
   from `pre-use-check`. One line on each scope type: the image carried by fibres to an eyepiece,
   or by a chip at the tip (S1 PDF 90–99).
4. **The tip face.** Lens, light guides, channel outlet; where a tool enters the picture.
5. **The channel decides what fits.** The two-diameters figure moves here. A tool in the channel
   stiffens the tip, limits its bend and cuts suction (S1 PDF 90–99; Kronborg 2024).
6. **Tool families.** Forceps types, the brush and the protected brush, the needle (S1; Judge
   2014). One line each on the cryoprobe, basket and balloon (E01-O2; U11, T04, T14), their
   training named, not taught.
7. **Passing a tool.** The instrument lab and its card move here from `biopsy-and-specimens`.
8. **Which tool for which job.** A picture task: the target, then the tool and its state.
9. **Check.** New details: a tool whose fit or state is in doubt.
10. **Close.** The checklist again.

About ten minutes.

## Sources it would add

- **Judge EP et al.** Anatomy and bronchoscopy of the porcine lung: a model for translational
  respiratory medicine. Am J Respir Cell Mol Biol 2014;51(3):334–343. doi:10.1165/rcmb.2013-0453TR.
  Brush and forceps types, page 340. Already registered as U24 with the lab screen.
- **Kronborg SH et al.** Four different models for simulation-based training of bronchoscopic
  procedures. BMC Pulm Med 2024;24:23. doi:10.1186/s12890-024-02846-9. With a tool in the
  channel, only one of four models let every operator reach RB1; tissue sampling worked only in
  the porcine lung and the Thiel cadaver. The first author works for a scope maker (Ambu); its
  register row should say so.
- **Fielding DI et al.** Achieving competency in bronchoscopy: challenges and opportunities.
  Respirology 2014;19(4):472–482. doi:10.1111/resp.12279. Simulation before patients; it supports
  the lab-then-bench order, not any technique.
